# Results and interpretation

The held-out technical pilot contains only 10 identifier and 2 natural queries. On identifier queries, TF-IDF, MiniLM and RRF have MRR@10 of 0.733, 0.813 and 0.875 respectively; Recall@5 is 1.0, 0.9 and 1.0. The selected proposed and selected match-only methods equal RRF in both scores and ranking by construction because their selected weights are zero. The three methods' wrong-ID bounds are 1/10 to 2/10: one observed error and one conflicting unjudged result.

The nonzero exploratory match-only and full variants each have identifier MRR@10 1.0 and Recall@5 1.0, with 0/10 observed or unknown wrong-ID cases. Their paired mean MRR difference versus RRF is +0.125, with a 95% percentile bootstrap interval [0.000, 0.300], 10,000 resamples, seed 42 and only 10 queries. These are descriptive pilot results, not a validated general improvement. The exploratory match-only and full metrics are identical on this sample, so this experiment does not demonstrate an incremental benefit from the conflict penalty. No fixed-variant deterioration in per-query MRR is observed in this small sample; this is not evidence that none occurs elsewhere.

The case table is generated from saved rankings. For test01, ordinary RRF placed an explicitly negative conflicting CVE first and the relevant cross-referencing CVE second; the exploratory full variant placed that known positive first. For test04, the selected no-op left the known relevant CVE at rank four; the exploratory full setting moved it to rank one. For test11, an identifier-free natural query, both variants kept the entire RRF order unchanged. The first case's relevant top result can have a different CVE in its document title because it explicitly references the queried issue; the curated evidence records this relevance decision. These examples are selected to explain behavior, not a sample of independent validation.

The figures and CSV/LaTeX tables are generated directly from `outputs/final/` by `scripts/make_paper_artifacts.py`. Wrong-ID uncertainty uses bounds, not false zeroes for unjudged conflicts. Timing plots separate warmed component samples from one-time model/index costs and do not add them into an unmeasured end-to-end number. The optional SciFact run is a general-domain robustness check only; it does not validate technical identifier accuracy.
